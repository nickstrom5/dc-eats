Injera, tibs and wat

The District's Ethiopian restaurants cluster around U Street and Shaw, with more in Adams Morgan, Columbia Heights and up Georgia Avenue. A meal comes on a round of injera, the spongy teff flatbread, topped with stews (wat), sautéed meat (tibs) and lentils and greens, and you eat it by tearing off more injera. Eritrean places serve close cousins of the same dishes.

The list at a glance

Neighborhoods with the most: Shaw (4), Adams Morgan (2), Petworth (2), Columbia Heights (2), Near Northeast (1), Pleasant Plains (1), Dupont Circle (1).

Our method: a place made the list only if a source named it for the food and a 2025 or 2026 source showed it open (its own site or menu, a dated local story, the MICHELIN Guide, a current Washington.org page, or its own active DC business license), and its street address matched. No current source, or a website gone dark, and it stayed off. Review sites played no part, and places run by ward and neighborhood rather than by anybody's score. Carryouts and market stalls keep short hours, so check before you go.
