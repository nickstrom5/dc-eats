Built around how DC eats

Star ratings won't tell you which carryout still does the half-smoke all the way, or which Ethiopian kitchen is open late. DC Eats is organized the way people here eat: Ethiopian restaurants, half-smokes, mumbo sauce carryouts, jumbo slice, pupuserías and seafood at the Wharf, each place checked in fall 2026 against a 2025–26 source. Each place shows its own DC business and alcohol licenses, ABCA's outdoor-seating endorsements make a Sidewalk Cafés list, and Apple Maps' live card supplies ratings, hours and photos.

Free, with no catch

The app costs nothing, shows no ads and sells nothing inside, and there's no account to make. The lists ship inside the app, so they open right away and keep working when the signal drops out on the Metro.

What stays on your phone

DC Eats collects nothing about you: no sign-in, no analytics, no ads. Location, if you turn it on, only puts the nearest places first, and your saved places are kept on the phone or iPad. The website has no cookies and no trackers. The privacy policy has the details.

Questions

Is DC Eats free?

Completely: no price, no ads, no in-app purchases and no account to create. It's an iPhone and iPad app on its way to the App Store, with no Android version yet; until it's out, the web search here works in any browser.

Where do the lists come from?

Two kinds of source. The Ethiopian, half-smoke, mumbo sauce, jumbo slice, pupusa and fish market lists are our own research from fall 2026, with a source naming each place for the food and a 2025 or 2026 source showing it open: its website or menu, a dated local story, the MICHELIN Guide, a current Washington.org page or its own active DC business license. The wider directory of 2,974 restaurants starts from Overture Maps' open place data, keeps the kinds of listing that proved reliable against the District's own licenses, and adds licensed restaurants and bars the map data missed.

Where can I get Ethiopian food in DC?

Our Ethiopian list has 13 hand-checked places in 7 neighborhoods, more than half of them in Shaw, Adams Morgan and Columbia Heights. In the web search, the list puts the nearest first once you share your location.

Does DC grade its restaurants?

No. DC Health inspects food establishments pass/fail and gives no letter grade, score or placard. Its reports are posted in its own search, which each place links; we copy none and compute no grade.

Does it show which places serve alcohol?

Yes, from the District's own records: a place serves alcohol when it holds a live on-premises license on ABCA's roster (1,304 places we list). Without one, it shows as unknown, never as no. ABCA's sidewalk café and summer garden endorsements feed the Sidewalk Cafés list.

Does it show ratings and reviews?

Not from us. Tapping a place opens Apple Maps' own card inside the app, with Apple's current ratings, hours, photos and directions. None of our lists is sorted by anyone's rating.

Does it need my location?

No. Location only matters for putting the nearest places first. If you share it, it stays on your iPhone or iPad and never reaches us, and the app works fine without it. On this website, Near me reads your location inside your browser and nowhere else.

A place has closed, or something's wrong. How do I tell you?

Write to nick@eatsranked.com with the place's name, its neighborhood and what changed. Corrections ship with the next data update.
